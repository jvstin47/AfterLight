# Audio Design & Cinematic Soundscape Reference

**Project:** AFTERLIGHT  
**Setting:** Apollo 12 Landing Site (Statio Cognitum / Oceanus Procellarum)  
**Atmosphere:** Hard vacuum (true silence external to spacesuits and spacecraft hull)

---

## 1. Acoustic Philosophy: The Architecture of Silence
In an airless vacuum, sound waves cannot propagate through the environment. The soundscape of the lunar surface is profoundly intimate, internal, and mechanical.

To achieve cinematic power without resorting to generic sci-fi tropes, AFTERLIGHT will employ two distinct auditory layers:

### Layer A: The Internal Human Acoustic Sphere (Inside the A7L Spacesuit)
1. **Suit Environmental Control System (ECS):**
   - Constant, low-frequency white/pink noise drone of oxygen circulation fans (approx. 240–320 Hz).
   - Occasional rhythmic clicking of pressure relief valves and sublimator water evaporation cycles.
2. **Physiological Sounds:**
   - Close-mic breathing (slow rhythmic inhales, labored exhales during steep crater climbs).
   - Cardiac pulse audible during moments of physical exertion.
3. **Bone & Suit Conduction:**
   - Heavy, dull, thudding vibrations transmitted through the soles of lunar overshoes into the astronaut's skeleton with each bounding stride.
   - Mechanical clinks and grating vibrations conducted through suit gloves when grasping metallic core tubes, hammer handles, or bolt cutters.

### Layer B: The Machine Voice (Radio & Telemetry)
1. **Quindar Tones:**
   - Intro beep: 2,525 Hz sine tone (duration 250 ms), triggered whenever Mission Control (CapCom) opened the push-to-talk line.
   - Outro beep: 2,475 Hz sine tone (duration 250 ms), triggered upon release.
2. **VHF Radio Telemetry Grain:**
   - 10-bit PCM split-phase telemetry carrier hiss and subtle phase jitter.
   - S-band Doppler frequency drift and tape flutter characteristic of NASA voice loop recordings.
3. **The Seismic Voice (Passive Seismic Experiment):**
   - Transposing the ALSEP PSE seismic signals (recorded at 0.1 to 10 Hz) into the human audible range (pitch-shifted up 100x).
   - The eerie, resonant reverberations of the lunar crust ringing for over an hour following the LM ascent stage crash.

## 2. Audio Reference Logs (Apollo 12 Flight Recordings)
- **NASA Audio Archive:** Master Tape 12-048 (EVA-2 Surveyor crater traverse).
- **Historic Voice Artifact:** Pete Conrad's laughing vocal delivery and Alan Bean's technical commentary as they dismantle the Surveyor camera.
- **Tone & Mood:** Contrast between playful human camaraderie in 1969 and the solemn, eternal quiet of the abandoned hardware in the present day.
