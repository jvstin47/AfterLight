## 1. Historical & Scientific Significance
On November 20, 1969, Apollo 12 astronauts Pete Conrad and Alan Bean retrieved key engineering components from the robotic Surveyor 3 lander, which had been exposed on the lunar surface for **31 months (974 Earth days)**.

This represents humanity's first controlled forensic recovery and examination of man-made materials subjected to an extraterrestrial vacuum environment.

---

## 2. Retrieved Components Inventory
1. **Television Camera Unit:** Weight ~7.8 kg, consisting of the vidicon tube, optics, filter wheel assembly, elevation/azimuth drive mechanisms, electronic circuit boards, and thermal shroud.
2. **Soil Mechanics Surface Sampler (SMSS) Scoop:** Aluminum scoop bucket with motor drive gearbox and carbide cutting blade.
3. **Landing Leg 3 Strut Tubing:** Unpainted 7075-T6 aluminum alloy tubular structural member.
4. **Electrical Cabling:** Multiconductor wiring bundles wrapped in Kapton and Teflon thermal insulation.

---

## 3. Key Scientific and Forensic Findings

### A. Optical Degradation and "Browning" Effect
- The white inorganic thermal control paint (titanium dioxide in potassium silicate binder) had turned a noticeable brownish-tan hue.
- Forensic analysis revealed two independent mechanisms:
  1. **Solar UV Radiation Photolysis:** Prolonged exposure to unfiltered solar ultraviolet radiation created color centers and altered the chemical valence of the pigment.
  2. **Intrepid Descent Engine Sandblasting:** High-velocity lunar dust particles ejected by the TRW Descent Engine of LM Intrepid during touchdown 163 meters away sandblasted the camera housing, scouring off microscopic surface coatings and embedding micro-shards of basaltic regolith into the metal surfaces facing northwest.

### B. Micrometeoroid Impact Flux
- Detailed optical microscopic examination of the camera shroud and polished optical mirror revealed dozens of micro-craters ranging from 1 to 100 microns in diameter.
- **Scientific Conclusion:** The lunar micrometeoroid bombardment rate was significantly lower than conservative pre-Apollo models had assumed, demonstrating that long-duration spacecraft hulls require less massive shielding than previously estimated.

### C. The *Streptococcus mitis* Controversy
- In 1970, NASA biologists announced that 50 to 100 viable cells of *Streptococcus mitis* (a benign oral bacterium) were cultured from a small piece of polyurethane foam insulation inside the camera circuit boards, leading to sensational claims that terrestrial bacteria had survived 31 months of lunar vacuum, radiation, and temperature swings.
- Subsequent rigorous investigations (conducted in the late 1990s and 2000s) proved that the finding was a **false positive resulting from clean-room contamination**. Photographic and video logs from the 1970 teardown showed technicians working without proper surgical masks, with flow hoods operated in configurations that allowed ambient airborne biological contamination onto the sterile culture plates.
