# Apollo 12 Mission Report Summary (MSC-01855)

**Document ID:** NASA MSC-01855 / NTRS 19760072997  
**Publication Date:** March 1970  
**Originating Center:** NASA Manned Spacecraft Center (Houston, Texas)  
**Primary Archive Copy:** [Apollo_12_Mission_Report_MSC-01855.pdf](./Apollo_12_Mission_Report_MSC-01855.pdf)

---

## 1. Mission Overview & Key Flight Events
- **Launch Date:** November 14, 1969, 16:22:00 UTC from LC-39A, Kennedy Space Center, Florida.
- **Atmospheric Lightning Strike:** At T+36.5 seconds and T+52 seconds after liftoff, the Saturn V rocket was struck by cloud-to-ground atmospheric electrical discharges triggered by the ionized exhaust plume. The main electrical buses dropped offline, tumbling telemetry. EECOM John Aaron delivered the historic call: *"Flight, try SCE to AUX."* Pilot Alan Bean flipped the Spacecraft Control Electronics switch to auxiliary, restoring power distribution and saving the mission.
- **Translunar Injection (TLI):** S-IVB burn placed Apollo 12 into a hybrid non-free-return translunar trajectory.
- **Lunar Landing:** November 19, 1969, at 06:54:35 UTC. Lunar Module *Intrepid* (LM-6) touched down at 3.01239° S, 23.42157° W in Oceanus Procellarum.
- **Landing Proximity:** Touchdown occurred precisely 163 meters (535 ft) northwest of the robotic Surveyor 3 spacecraft, executing the Apollo program's first pinpoint precision landing.

## 2. Extravehicular Activities (EVAs) Summary

### EVA-1 (November 19, 1969)
- **Duration:** 3 hours, 56 minutes, 3 seconds.
- **Objectives Accomplished:**
  - Commander Pete Conrad and LMP Alan Bean egressed the LM cabin.
  - Conrad's first step quote: *"Whoopee! Man, that may have been a small one for Neil, but that's a long one for me!"*
  - Deployed Modular Equipment Stowage Assembly (MESA) and color TV camera (which failed shortly after deployment when accidentally pointed into direct sunlight).
  - Unloaded ALSEP subpackages 1 and 2 from LM Quadrant IV.
  - Alan Bean extracted the Plutonium-238 fuel capsule from the graphite cask using the Hanford extraction tool and inserted it into the SNAP-27 RTG.
  - Carried ALSEP packages ~130 meters northwest and deployed Central Station, Passive Seismic Experiment (PSE), Lunar Surface Magnetometer (LSM), Solar Wind Spectrometer (SWS), and Suprathermal Ion Detector Experiment (SIDE) / Cold Cathode Gauge Experiment (CCGE).
  - Planted the United States flag.
  - Collected contingency and bulk regolith samples (16.7 kg).

### EVA-2 (November 20, 1969)
- **Duration:** 3 hours, 48 minutes, 42 seconds.
- **Objectives Accomplished:**
  - Geologic traverse covering ~1.48 km visiting a chain of impact craters: Middle Crescent Crater, Head Crater (Stations 1-3), Bench Crater (Station 4), Sharp Crater (Station 5, turnaround point), and Halo Crater (Station 6).
  - Visited Surveyor Crater and descended the 11-14° inner slope to the Surveyor 3 spacecraft (Station 7).
  - Thoroughly inspected Surveyor 3's resting orientation, surface footpad impressions, and soil disturbance.
  - Cut and unbolted target hardware:
    1. The Television Camera Unit.
    2. The mechanical Soil Sampler Scoop.
    3. Approximately 20 cm of unpainted aluminum tubing from landing leg 3 strut.
    4. Two sections of electrical cabling.
  - Collected 17.6 kg of documented samples, including core tube samples and rock sample 12013 (a highly differentiated granite-like lithology).

## 3. Ascent and Intentional Seismic Impact
- **Lunar Liftoff:** November 20, 1969, at 14:25:47 UTC. Total surface duration: 31 hours, 31 minutes, 12 seconds.
- **Ascent Stage Deorbit Impact:** After rendezvous and crew transfer back to the Command Module *Yankee Clipper*, the empty ascent stage was purposefully commanded to retrofire and crash into the lunar surface 72 km east-southeast of the landing site.
- **Impact Coordinates:** 3.94° S, 21.20° W.
- **Seismic Discovery:** The impact created a 20-meter crater and generated continuous seismic vibrations recorded by the newly deployed ALSEP Passive Seismic Experiment that resonated for over an hour, prompting geophysicist Dr. Maurice Ewing to describe the Moon as *"ringing like a bell."*
