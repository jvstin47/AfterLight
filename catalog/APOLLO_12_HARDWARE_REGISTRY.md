# Apollo 12 Hardware Registry: The Machines That Stayed Behind

**Site:** Statio Cognitum / Oceanus Procellarum, The Moon  
**Coordinates:** 3.01239° S, 23.42157° W  
**Landing Date:** November 19, 1969 (06:54:35 UTC)  
**Surface Stay Duration:** 31 hours, 31 minutes, 12 seconds  

This document serves as the authoritative, definitive historical registry of all human machinery, scientific instruments, structural stages, and discarded equipment currently resting on the lunar surface at the Apollo 12 site.

---

## 1. Spacecraft & Heavy Structures

### 1.1 Intrepid Lunar Module Descent Stage (LM-6)
- **Manufacturer:** Grumman Aerospace Corporation (Bethpage, New York).
- **Coordinates:** 3.01239° S, 23.42157° W.
- **Physical Envelope:** 3.23 m height, 4.22 m width across flats, 9.45 m diagonal landing gear span.
- **Dry Mass:** 2,211 kg.
- **Components Remaining at Site:**
  - Octagonal welded aluminum space frame wrapped in multi-layer Kapton (gold and amber aluminized mylar) thermal insulation.
  - Four deployable landing gear struts featuring aluminum honeycomb crushable shock-absorption cartridges inside primary tubular cylinders.
  - Four 0.94 m diameter dish-shaped footpads resting on basaltic regolith.
  - TRW Descent Propulsion System (DPS) gimbal ring, thrust chamber, and columbium radiation-cooled nozzle extension bell.
  - Two Aerozine-50 fuel propellant spherical titanium tanks and two Nitrogen Tetroxide (N2O4) oxidizer spherical titanium tanks.
  - Supercritical helium storage cryo-tank.
  - Forward egress ladder mounted on Quad I strut.
  - Stainless steel commemorative plaque affixed between ladder rungs: *"Here Men From The Planet Earth First Set Foot Upon The Moon, November 1969 A.D. We Came In Peace For All Mankind."* Signed by Charles Conrad Jr., Richard F. Gordon Jr., Alan L. Bean, and President Richard Nixon.
  - Lunar surface sensing probes (1.68 m long wires attached to footpads 1, 2, and 4; severed and folded upon touchdown).
  - Empty Modular Equipment Stowage Assembly (MESA) chassis in Quad II.
  - Empty scientific equipment pallet bays in Quad III and Quad IV.

### 1.2 Surveyor 3 Spacecraft
- **Manufacturer:** Hughes Aircraft Company / NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).
- **Launch Date:** April 17, 1967. Touchdown: April 20, 1967.
- **Coordinates:** 3.0152° S, 23.4189° W (~163 m southeast of LM *Intrepid*).
- **Location Context:** Resting on an 11° to 14° inner slope inside the 200-meter-wide Surveyor Crater.
- **Landed Mass:** ~294 kg (initial landed mass; ~280 kg remaining after Apollo 12 hardware retrieval).
- **Components Remaining at Site:**
  - Triangular space frame constructed of tubular thin-wall 7075-T6 aluminum alloy.
  - Three hinged landing gear legs and 0.29 m diameter crushable aluminum honeycomb footpads.
  - Central vertical mast (height 3.0 m).
  - High-gain planar array antenna (0.80 m x 0.50 m) mounted on mast gimbal.
  - Solar panel array (1.0 m x 0.7 m) containing 3,960 solar cells atop mast.
  - Two deployable omnidirectional antenna booms (Omni A and Omni B).
  - Thermal Compartment A (transponders, flight electronics) and Thermal Compartment B (central logic, power conditioners).
  - Three liquid vernier rocket engines (throttled monomethylhydrazine/MON-10).
  - Spherical propellant tanks (2 fuel, 1 oxidizer) and spherical helium tank.
  - Jettisoned solid retro-rocket attachment ring.
  - *Retrieved Hardware Void:* Empty mounting bracket on Leg 2 where the TV camera was unbolted and cut; severed scissor-arm strut where the soil sampler scoop was detached; cut tube segment on Leg 3 strut.

## 2. Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP Array A)
**Location:** ~130 meters northwest of LM *Intrepid* (3.0116° S, 23.4230° W).  
**Operational History:** Nov 19, 1969 – Sept 30, 1977 (7 years, 10 months continuous telemetry).

### 2.1 ALSEP Central Station (CS)
- **Manufacturer:** Bendix Aerospace Systems Division.
- **Dimensions:** 0.64 m x 0.61 m x 0.58 m box. Mass: ~25 kg.
- **Artifact Details:** Aluminum enclosure housing the command receiver, PCM telemetry encoder, data processor, power conditioning unit, and thermal dissipation louvers. S-band helical antenna (1.2 m mast) pointed toward Earth; sun compass gnomon dial on top plate; flat ribbon multi-conductor cables radiating outward across the regolith to each experiment.

### 2.2 SNAP-27 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG)
- **Manufacturer:** General Electric Space Division for the Atomic Energy Commission.
- **Location:** 3.0 meters from Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Beryllium cylindrical casing with 8 radial cooling fins (height 0.46 m, diameter 0.40 m). Contains 3.8 kg of Plutonium-238 Dioxide microspheres. Operated at ~590°C, producing 73.5 Watts of DC power at deployment (decaying to 63W at shutdown in 1977). The Pu-238 heat source remains active today (half-life: 87.7 years; currently emitting ~480W thermal).

### 2.3 Passive Seismic Experiment (PSE)
- **Principal Investigator:** Dr. Gary Latham (Lamont-Doherty Geological Observatory).
- **Location:** 2.7 meters from Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Three long-period seismometers and one short-period vertical seismometer housed inside a white 10-sided aluminized Mylar/Kapton specular thermal shroud (base diameter 0.9 m). Connected to Central Station via a flat 5-meter ribbon cable.

### 2.4 Lunar Surface Magnetometer (LSM)
- **Principal Investigator:** Dr. Palmer Dyal (NASA Ames Research Center).
- **Location:** 15.0 meters from Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Three orthogonal fluxgate magnetometer sensor heads positioned at the tips of three 100-cm boom arms radiating from a central fiberglass mast, leveled on a tripod base.

### 2.5 Solar Wind Spectrometer (SWS)
- **Principal Investigator:** Dr. Conway Snyder (Jet Propulsion Laboratory).
- **Location:** 4.0 meters from Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Hexagonal housing supporting seven hemispherical Faraday cups oriented upward to sample incoming solar plasma electrons and protons.

### 2.6 Suprathermal Ion Detector Experiment (SIDE) & Cold Cathode Gauge (CCGE)
- **Principal Investigator:** Dr. John Freeman (Rice University).
- **Location:** 15.5 meters from Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Dual instrument package on a small deployment pallet. Measures low-energy positive ions and lunar ambient atmospheric gas pressure (down to 10^-12 torr) with a wire mesh screen and curved plate analyzer.

### 2.7 Lunar Dust Detector
- **Location:** Mounted directly on top plate of the ALSEP Central Station.
- **Artifact Details:** Three small solar cells mounted at different angles to measure dust accumulation caused by the ascent stage liftoff blast.

## 3. Discarded EVA Hardware & Jettisoned Equipment

### 3.1 Jettisoned Life Support Systems (Post-EVA-2 Cabin Cleanout)
- **Portable Life Support System (PLSS) Backpacks:** Two Apollo A7L PLSS units (PLSS 1 - Pete Conrad, PLSS 2 - Alan Bean). Discarded through the LM forward hatch onto the regolith directly beneath the egress ladder to shed 68 kg of earth-weight before lunar orbit insertion.
- **Oxygen Purge Systems (OPS):** Emergency high-pressure oxygen backpacks and helmet attachment brackets discarded with PLSS units.
- **Lunar Overshoes (Boots):** Two pairs of silicone-soled overshoes with blue woven Chromel-R fabric uppers, discarded after EVA-2.
- **Extravehicular Gloves & Visors:** Used EVA thermal gloves and outer helmet visors.
- **Defecation & Urine Collection Assemblies:** Jettisoned biomedical waste bags.

### 3.2 Lunar Surface Tools & Geological Field Equipment
- **Apollo Lunar Hand Tool Carrier (ALHTC):** Collapsible aluminum hand-cart frame used to carry core tubes, hammers, and sample bags.
- **Extension Handle & Tongs:** Stainless steel geological sample collection tongs and extension rods.
- **Adjustable Trenching Tool / Scoop:** Geological digging scoop.
- **Geological Hammer:** Hardened steel sampling hammer with aluminum handle.
- **Gnomon with Photometric Chart:** Tripod-mounted free-swinging weighted gnomon rod with gray-scale and color reflectance chart, left at sampling Station 4.
- **Core Drill Stems:** Stainless steel and titanium hollow core tubes used for soil stratification sampling.
- **Solar Wind Composition (SWC) Experiment Staff:** Aluminum deployment staff (the platinum/aluminum collector foil sheet was rolled up and returned; the mast remained planted in the soil).
- **Hasselblad 500EL 70mm Camera Bodies:** Two camera bodies (film magazines returned; camera bodies and lenses discarded to save weight).
- **Westinghouse Color Lunar TV Camera:** Discarded near the LM after its vidicon sensor was burned out by direct sunlight exposure on EVA-1.

## 4. Memorial, Cultural & Symbolic Artifacts

### 4.1 The United States Lunar Surface Flag
- **Dimensions:** 3 x 5 ft (0.91 x 1.52 m) nylon flag.
- **Mounting:** Two-piece telescoping aluminum staff with horizontal spreader arm along top edge.
- **Location:** Planted ~7 meters south of *Intrepid* by Pete Conrad and Alan Bean during EVA-1.
- **Current Condition:** Decades of intense unfiltered solar UV radiation and thermal cycling have bleached the nylon fabric pure white; mechanical degradation from solar wind bombardment may have fragmented portions of the weave.

### 4.2 Astronaut Commemorative Items
- **Silver Astronaut Pin:** Left on the surface by Pete Conrad in memory of astronaut Clifton "C.C." Williams, who was killed in a T-38 jet crash in 1967 and was originally slated to be the Lunar Module Pilot on Apollo 12.
- **Naval Aviation Wings:** Conrad and Bean, both decorated US Navy commanders, left small naval insignia on the regolith.
