EXPERIMENT 04 • ARCHIVAL EDITORIAL

Twice to the Surface of the Ocean of Storms

On November 19, 1969, at 06:54:35 UTC, Pete Conrad and Alan Bean touched down on the basalt plains of Oceanus Procellarum. Their coordinates were not chosen at random: 163 meters away, resting on the sloping rim of a 200-meter crater, sat Surveyor 3—a robotic vanguard that had preceded human arrival by two and a half years.

"Look at that Surveyor, sitting right on the contour... It’s bright, but it’s sitting right there where it belongs."
— Pete Conrad, CDR (Nov 19, 1969, 115:22:18 GET)

The mission demonstrated that Apollo could land with pinpoint precision. Conrad and Bean conducted two EVAs totaling 7 hours and 45 minutes, traversing 1.3 kilometers of cratered regolith. They dismantled Surveyor 3's television camera, cutting its aluminum cables with hand shears to investigate how materials endure decades in solar radiation and micro-meteoroid flux.
